# Gender-Based Violence in Rajasthan: Latest Available District Analysis (2024)

## Executive summary

The latest NCRB year identified for this analysis is 2024. Rajasthan recorded **36,563 registered cases of crimes against women** in 2024, down from 45,450 in 2023. This is a **19.6% decrease** in recorded cases year over year. The 2024 district totals cover 41 districts and sum exactly to 36,563.

- Jaipur had the highest count at **3,646** (10.0% of Rajasthan’s total), followed by Alwar (2,097), Bharatpur (1,556), Jodhpur (1,394), and Bhilwara (1,337). The top five account for 27.4% of registered cases.
- The largest selected recorded offence head was **cruelty by husband or relatives** (10,578; 28.9% of the aggregate), followed by assault with intent to outrage modesty (8,276; 22.6%), rape (4,871; 13.3%), and kidnapping/abduction of women (4,042; 11.1%).
- These are police-registered cases, not a count of all incidents. The decrease does not establish that actual violence fell by 19.6%; reporting and registration can change too.

## State-level trend

From 2021 to 2024, recorded cases decreased by 10.2%. The 2021–2023 state totals come from the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) parliamentary answer citing NCRB *Crime in India*. The 2024 number comes from the NCRB-derived district service and reconciles exactly to its 41 Rajasthan district records.

## What this suggests for Vishakha

1. Treat Jaipur’s high absolute caseload as a prompt to examine support-service capacity and referrals, not as a safety ranking.
2. The high count under cruelty by husband or relatives supports attention to accessible counselling, legal assistance, shelter, and referral pathways for violence within family relationships.
3. Compare police counts with anonymized programme and referral information, where available, to identify gaps between reported cases and support received.
4. Ask district teams and community partners whether the recorded decrease reflects changes in incidents, reporting access, awareness, registration, or classification before setting programme priorities.
5. For ongoing monitoring, pair crime data with service access indicators such as time to first support, referrals completed, and survivor feedback, with consent and privacy safeguards.

## Method and data quality

- **District data:** NCRB-derived “District Wise Crime Against Women” feature service, using 2024 aggregate field `totcrwom24` and crime-head fields. It includes district names/codes and 41 Rajasthan districts. District totals sum to **36,563**.
- **State trend:** MHA Rajya Sabha answer No. 390, “State/UT-wise Cases Registered under Crime against Women during 2021–2023,” for the 2021–2023 values. For 2024, the district-service aggregate is used; it matches the sum of district records.
- **Boundary and year caveat:** The district layer provides a current district structure. This report uses one year for district comparisons and does not calculate rates from 2011 Census populations, which would not align sufficiently with current boundaries and year.
- **BNS transition:** 2024 includes BNS as well as IPC crime-head fields following the new criminal laws. Legal categories and recording may not be directly comparable across years. The 2024 state trend is shown for context, not as a definitive measure of underlying prevalence change.
- **Counts, not prevalence:** All findings describe cases registered with police. They do not capture unreported incidents and are influenced by reporting access, willingness, registration practices, and classification.

## Ethical interpretation

Do not interpret a low count as proof of safety or a high count as proof that one district is more dangerous. Do not infer causes from these district-level figures. Use aggregated information only and do not attempt to identify survivors or communities.
